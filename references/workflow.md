# Workflow and Decision Rules

## Shared lifecycle

Use these phases as a lightweight control loop. Revisit an earlier phase when evidence shows a decision was wrong; do not treat gates as bureaucracy.

| Phase | Main work | Reviewable output | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | Clarify purpose, audience, context, constraints, success, and unknowns | Project brief; facts/decisions/assumptions/open questions | Creator agrees on direction and priority questions are resolved or explicitly deferred |
| Define | Bound the scope; specify deliverables, versions, requirements, dependencies, and acceptance | Requirements/deliverables matrix; risk list | High-impact requirements are testable and ownership is clear |
| Design | Build learning architecture and audiovisual blueprint | Series bible if needed; episode design brief; evidence map; script outline | Goals, content scope, format, teaching approach, and platform plan are coherent |
| Prototype | Test uncertain or expensive choices in a representative sample | Style frame, animatic, or short end-to-end sample | Creator accepts the direction and key risks have a mitigation |
| Produce | Create in reviewable batches against the approved blueprint | Script, storyboard, media, edit, derivatives | Each batch passes its agreed review before dependent work expands |
| Verify | Review instructional, factual, creative, technical, accessibility, rights, and platform fit | Issue log; acceptance record; final deliverables | Required checks pass or explicit exceptions are accepted and documented |
| Prepare release | Assemble files, metadata, captions, thumbnails, and platform settings | Release checklist/package and preview evidence | All items are ready for creator's specific publication confirmation |
| Learn and maintain | Review audience/learning feedback, production costs, and changes needed | Retrospective; update proposals; next-episode links | Reusable rules and next actions are recorded |

## Discovery questions

Ask in small batches, usually no more than three to five high-impact questions per turn. Prioritize answers that could change audience, learning goals, content scope, production approach, cost, rights, or deliverable variants.

Cover as relevant:

- Purpose and desired audience outcome; primary audience and prerequisite knowledge.
- Use context: classroom, self-study, public feed, course module, or other.
- Topic boundaries, required and excluded claims, authoritative sources, and review owner.
- Single/series/ongoing scale; how episodes relate and what remains stable.
- Presenter arrangement: one person, dialogue, narration, character-led, or mixed; why it serves learning.
- Character/persona and visual continuity needs, if any.
- Opening, recurring identity, ending, recap, question, and next-episode answer/return plan.
- Teaching strategies, tone, runtime range, pacing, visual density, audio, captions, and accessibility.
- Distribution platforms, aspect ratios, versions, localization, file handoff, and metadata.
- Time/budget/tools/skills, permissions, media rights, review cycle, and AI autonomy boundaries.
- Success evidence: what the viewer should explain, distinguish, solve, or remember; how it will be checked.

Use a decision table with `ID`, `question`, `options`, `recommendation`, `reason`, `impact if deferred`, `owner`, and `status`. Mark unknown platform requirements for current official-source verification at production/release time.

## Blueprint gate before substantial production

Confirm a project/series blueprint and episode design brief before producing a full script, large asset set, or polished edit. At minimum they describe:

- audience, context, learning outcomes, prerequisites, scope, exclusions, and evidence basis;
- sequence/argument arc and section purpose;
- presenter count/roles, persona and relationship, performance rules, and visual design if applicable;
- opening hook and recurring intro treatment; ending, recap, retrieval question, CTA/preview, and any cross-episode answer;
- chosen teaching methods and their placement, with rationale and observable evidence;
- visual system, diagrams/metaphors and their limits, sound approach, captions, and accessibility;
- platform/version matrix with aspect ratio, runtime target, safe composition, caption and packaging needs;
- risks, dependencies, acceptance checks, and the representative sample needed to validate uncertainty.

This is a completeness gate, not a demand to decide every shot in advance. Defer low-impact shot details to storyboard/prototype, where they can be evaluated cheaply.

## Task handoff and persistent state

Give each substantial task an ID and state: goal, inputs with version, output path/format, requirements, acceptance evidence, permitted autonomy, dependencies, and unresolved questions. Require the AI to report output versions, checks performed, failures/limits, assumptions, and next action.

Keep a compact project status document current with: scale, current phase, current approved baseline, active episode, in-progress/blocked items, open decisions, latest review, and next action. On session handoff, read this plus linked artifacts, then state the current objective and relevant versions before editing.

Task status may be `backlog`, `ready`, `in progress`, `review`, `blocked`, `changes requested`, or `accepted`. Count accepted work items or accepted shots when useful; do not invent completion percentages.

## Change control

Record defects separately from improvements and scope changes:

- Defect: violates an agreed requirement or contains an error. Fix and check affected derivatives.
- In-scope refinement: improves an artifact without changing goals or delivery. Apply within delegated authority.
- Scope/decision change: changes audience, learning goal, factual position, story structure, presenter approach, platform set, rights, budget, or approved baseline. Assess impact before implementation.

For a scope change, show affected requirement IDs and downstream artifacts, what remains reusable, rework, cost/time/quality implications, risks, and alternatives. Update the baseline only after the authorized creator decision. Preserve prior accepted versions.

## Progressive delivery

For series, complete one representative episode or vertical slice through export and pre-release review before scaling the production pipeline. Then stabilize reusable templates and divide work into batches. Use feedback from each batch to adjust future work, while recording any series-wide rule change so episodes do not drift silently.
